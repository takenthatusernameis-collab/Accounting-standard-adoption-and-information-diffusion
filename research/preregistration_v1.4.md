## 0. Why this file exists

`research/preregistration_v1.3.md` §5.2 stated the expected cluster count for
PH-1 as **≈ 4–8**, from the five instruments that were primary-verified when
v1.3 was registered. Since then, **five further day-level instruments** have been
verified from primary sources (D-033, D-030, D-026): the Philippines, New
Zealand, Malaysia, Hong Kong SAR and Indonesia. The verified set is now **ten
primary-verified instruments in ten distinct T-A dates**, and the registered
expectation has moved with it.

A pre-registration whose stated expectation has been overtaken by the evidence is
a defect, and correcting it is the same act as correcting the false convergence
statement in v1.3 §5.2. **The rule that binds if the audit falls short does not
move**; only the prediction about what the audit will find is restated. The
≥ 12 minimum, the treatment-firm minimum of 200, the estimand, the test order,
the split rules and the reporting commitments are **untouched**.

**The hypothesis is not amended.** `research/hypothesis_v1.3.md` remains in force:
no claim, acceptance criterion or kill condition changes here.

### 5.2 Expected cluster counts (a prediction, not a result) — **restated again**

| Stratum | Expected clusters for PH-1 | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| A — content change (IFRS 18 in an existing framework) | **≈ 10–13** | One cluster per admitted jurisdiction with a verified condition-2 instrument, on the rule in `research/specification_v1.4.md` §1. **Ten** instruments are primary-verified today, with **ten distinct T-A dates** spanning 2024-05-10 to 2026-02-13: the Philippines, New Zealand, Malaysia, Hong Kong SAR, Singapore, Saudi Arabia, Indonesia, the United Kingdom, Brazil and the European Union. The EU is a **single** timing cohort, not twenty-seven independent ones. The **upper** end of the range allows for further jurisdictions whose own instruments are verified before the audit — the realistic candidates are the local-equivalent jurisdictions (South Korea, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Nepal, Chinese Taipei, Mongolia, Bhutan, Israel) and the EU member states whose carve-out check clears; the **lower** end assumes none of them clears and only the ten anchors are admitted. |
| B — framework change (first-time IFRS adopters) | **0** | Settled in advance, in the negative: first-time IFRS adopters apply the IFRSs "all at once", so the contrast degenerates and is not built. |
| **Total** | **≈ 10–13** | **Against the ≥ 12 minimum.** The arm is **no longer certainly inconclusive**, and it is **not yet shown to be feasible**: the decisive quantity is the number of jurisdictions that issue their **own dated IFRS 18 instrument**, and that is a handful, not a hundred. |

**What changed against v1.3 §5.2.** v1.3 stated **≈ 4–8** from five verified
instruments. Five further primary-verified instruments (D-033, D-030, D-026)
raise the verified count to **ten distinct dates**, and the plausible maximum is
now **bounded** by the number of jurisdictions that issue their own instrument
rather than open-ended.

**What did not change.** The **≥ 12 minimum** is not moved. A short arm binds the
conclusion, and the binding rule in §5.3 below is untouched. The **application**
date remains common at 2027-01-01 for every jurisdiction in the arm, with one
recorded exception: Thailand's IFRS 18 application date is **1 January 2028**
(primary-verified, S-96), so Thailand is **outside** the arm on a T-E ground and
is not counted in the range above.

### 5.3 The rule that binds if the audit falls short (frozen) — **unchanged**

> If the realised independent-cluster count for PH-1 is **< 12** or the treatment-
> firm count is **< 200**, the **PH-1 primary estimand is inconclusive by
> construction** — the same rule, and the same precedent, that
> `research/preregistration_v1.1.md` §5.3 already froze for the retrospective
> panel. The descriptive results are still produced for the record, and the
> decision is **park** or **inconclusive** — never "pass".
>
> **The rule is fixed here so that a short arm cannot later be used as a reason to
> change the panel, the window, or the thresholds.**

**Restated here unchanged on purpose.** v1.3 restated this rule; v1.4 restates
it again **verbatim**, because the expectation in §5.2 moved closer to the
threshold and a reader must be able to see at a glance that the threshold did
**not** move with it.

## 11. Status

PH-1 is **registered and untested**. It is blocked on the same authorisation items
as the retrospective panel (**A1** market data, **A2** read-only public harvesting,
**A4** panel scope) and on the verification of conditions 3 to 5. The number of
primary-verified day-level instruments has risen from five to **ten**, and the
expected cluster count with it, but **the gate is unchanged** and the arms are
**no less blocked** than they were.

Registering v1.4 does **not** unblock anything, and it does not license any
result. It fixes the test **before** the outcome exists, which is the only thing
it can do at this point. The campaign remains **PARKED — not validated, not
rejected.**
